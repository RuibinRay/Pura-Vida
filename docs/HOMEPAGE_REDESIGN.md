# Homepage redesign — audit, reference analysis, direction (2026-10-23)

## 1. Audit of the Milestone-1 homepage (live at ruibinray.github.io/Pura-Vida, inspected headless + screenshots)

What was weak, in order of importance:

1. **The hero carried the whole page and the page carried nothing else.** Below the 3D scene sat a plain contents list ("The journal") — a table of contents, not a journey. The brief's six-chapter story did not exist.
2. **The procedural forest read as "low-poly demo"**: lollipop hill trees, a flat clearing, a blocky sloth and toucan, a volcano cone with a cloud ring. Coherent, but a visitor compares it with photographs of Costa Rica and loses.
3. **No scroll language.** The only motion was the timed 13 s walk-in; scrolling did nothing. The brief asks for scroll-linked, reversible storytelling.
4. **Generic hero furniture**: a kicker, an H1, a tagline in quotes, two buttons, a "Doorways" card list — the pattern every template uses. The doorway links duplicated the contents list.
5. **Too much UI on the hero**: time-of-day select, sound, pause, skip, tour — five controls before the visitor has read a word.
6. **Typography was fine but undirected**: no chapter numbers, no scientific labels, the title wrapped at some widths.
7. **The still illustration (phone fallback) cropped badly** and looked like a different site.
8. **Performance was good on paper** (CLS 0, a11y 100) but the three.js chunk (256 KB gz) loaded before anything was readable, for a scene most visitors skip.

Kept: the Field-Journal identity (paper, ink, index tabs, stamps, status tags), the 3D relief map and its keyboard twin, the chapter pages, the sources system, the tier ladder and `?why`, trilingual UI.

## 2. Reference analysis

### Image A — the hero plate (rainforest, two toucans, waterfall, volcano, sloth, macaws, morpho; 1376×768, upscaled 2× with EDSR)
- **Composition.** A classic "window" composition: dark, detailed foliage frames the left, right and bottom edges; the eye travels from the toucans (top-left, the brightest colour) down the waterfall to the pool, then back up the valley to the volcano. The sloth closes the right edge; the macaws give the mid-ground scale; the morpho anchors the bottom-right.
- **Lighting.** Soft overcast daylight with warm sun on the toucans' branch and the far trees; mist sits in the valley and wraps the cone. Values: near frame dark, mid-ground mid-green, far hazy blue-grey — textbook atmospheric perspective, which is what makes it a natural 2.5D plate.
- **Colour.** Greens dominate (near: saturated dark; far: desaturated); accents are the toucans' yellow/green/red, the macaws' scarlet, the morpho's blue, the bromeliads' red. The palette is exactly the site's token set (forest, canopy, moss, turquoise, sunset, gold).
- **Depth / camera.** Three planes at roughly 1 m (frame), 50–200 m (waterfall, forest), 5–10 km (volcano). Eye level slightly above the pool. A scroll-linked dolly-in toward the volcano is the natural motion; the near frame should part and pass the camera, the mist should thin.
- **Typography placement.** The quietest region is the sky left of the cone and the mist band over the valley: the title sits there, left-aligned, over the sky, never over the animals.
- **Suggested motion.** Near frame: part left/right and scale up as we "walk in"; mist: drift; clouds: crawl across the summit; macaws: nothing (keep them still — moving cut-outs would look like stickers); morpho: an SVG morpho flies a slow figure-of-eight in front of the plate; toucans/sloth: respond to the pointer with a label and a slight lift.
- **Becoming a page.** Chapter 1 is this plate at rest; Chapter 2 is the same plate pushed into the volcano with the facts beside it; Chapter 3 rises the relief map out of the valley mist. One continuous shot, three chapters.

### Image B — the beach path (illustrated; sloth, capuchin, scarlet macaw, agouti, green iguana; 2752×1536)
- **Composition.** A tunnel of foliage opens onto a Pacific beach; the vanishing point is the horizon between the islands. Five animals sit on a ring around the opening: sloth and capuchin on the left trees, macaw on the right, agouti and iguana on the ground.
- **Style.** Ink outlines and flat painterly fills — a naturalist's plate rather than a photograph. That is the Field-Journal register, so it belongs on the wildlife chapter as an **interactive plate** with hotspots, not as a photo.
- **Lighting / colour.** Even daylight, cool sea against warm sand and greens; the animals are the saturated accents.
- **Motion.** A plate like this should barely move: a slow drift of the whole image with the pointer, hotspot rings that breathe, the animal regions lifting slightly on hover. Clicking an animal opens its journal entry (scientific name, IUCN status, habitat, diet, facts, sources). Habitat check: all five occur in Pacific lowland coastal forest (verified in research/species-coast.json).
- **Becoming a page.** Chapter 4 — "A world of wildlife" — and the header of the Wildlife page.

### Image C — the title poster (volcano over a lake, toucan, sloth, morpho, "COSTA RICA" baked in; 2752×1536)
- A finished poster: golden-hour light, the title set in a serif close to Literata over the mist band. Removing the title was tried (shift-map and FSR inpainting) and left visible ghosts, so the poster is used as what it is: **the end card** of the journey (Chapter 6, with a slow warm grade toward sunset) and the **social/OG image**. A title at the end of a film is a bookend, not a repetition.

## 3. Creative direction — "A journey through Costa Rica"

Three ways to realise the brief were weighed:
1. **One continuous plate, six chapters (chosen).** A single sticky stage; the hero photograph is the opening shot, the camera pushes in on scroll, the map rises out of the mist, the illustrated plate and the table are new "pages" of the same journal, and the poster closes. Scroll position is the only clock. Cheapest to load, reversible by construction, works without WebGL except the map.
2. **Full 3D rebuild of the plate** (GLTF foliage, volcano, water). Honest, but every model would be compared with the photograph beside it and lose; weeks of asset work for a worse hero.
3. **Separate full-screen scenes with snap scrolling.** The "disconnected sections" the brief rejects.

Technical choices: native `position: sticky` stage + one rAF scroll reader writing chapter progress (no GSAP — the existing R3F loop and CSS transforms already do the job, and one system means no conflicts); 2.5D layers cut from the plate with a near-frame mask (saturation × local contrast × a spatial prior), served as responsive WebP; the map's three.js chunk is loaded only when Chapter 2 is in view; reduced motion → static layers and crossfades; the still/text tiers show the plate without layers.

## 4. The walk (second redesign, 2026-10-23 evening)

The owner supplied seven more plates (Guanacaste dry forest at sunset with a white-tailed deer, scarlet macaws and a painted oxcart; Corcovado with a Baird's tapir, a scarlet macaw and a morpho; a Tortuguero mangrove channel with a keel-billed toucan, a morpho, a scarlet macaw, a three-toed sloth and a great blue heron; the Irazú crater above the Central Valley with a yigüirro and two hummingbirds; the Monteverde cloud forest with a resplendent quetzal, two hummingbirds, a morpho and a distant waterfall; Río Celeste; a Manuel Antonio sunset) and asked for the scroll to swap between them "as if walking into Costa Rica", for every animal to move in a 1–5 s loop, for the waterfall to flow, and for a "what if the volcano erupted".

**Structure.** Nine stops over the same sticky stage and driver: 01 Discover (the opening forest) → 02 Monteverde → 03 Río Celeste → 04 Irazú → 05 Guanacaste → 06 Flavors (the table) → 07 Corcovado → 08 Tortuguero → 09 The story continues (the Manuel Antonio sunset as the end card). The relief map was built as a stop between Irazú and Guanacaste (with the stops pinned on it and three country facts) and taken out on 2026-10-24 after the review: a dark map screen between two photographic plates broke the walk, and the owner had said to prefer smooth transitions over the map if it was not good; the map is one click away from every stop and the `kind: 'map'` support stays in the code. Each stop is a `Scene` (src/journey/Scene.tsx) driven by `--pn` (its own progress) and `--i`; the stop list is `src/journey/scenes.ts`. The earlier "Nature" facts chapter and the beach-path wildlife plate left the home (the facts moved onto the map stop and the place tiles; the beach plate stays on /wildlife).

**Transitions.** A stop fades in over the last ~10 % of the previous one, on top of it, arriving slightly larger and settling (scale 1.1 → 1), pushes in through its stop (→ 1.05) and keeps pushing as the next arrives — the forward motion carries through every dissolve. Misty stops add a breath of white at the boundary, the sunset ones a warm wash (`.scene__wash`, peaking at the boundary above both plates). In portrait the focal point pans across each plate as the stop progresses (`--pan-from → --pan-to`, clamped to the slack so the plate edge never shows), so every animal passes through the frame.

**Moving animals.** `tools/plates.py` cuts each animal out of its plate with U²-Net (salient-object segmentation on a crop, corrected by include/exclude polygons and GrabCut for antlers and dark wings), splits it into parts (a head, two wings about the body axis, the rest), inpaints the plate under every part (OpenCV FSR), and writes WebP layers with their boxes, pivots and axes into the manifest. The site animates the parts with CSS keyframes: heads turn/tilt/graze/watch about the neck, wings flap in 3D about the body axis in opposite phase (1.2 s), morpho wings fold (1.6 s), hummingbirds hover (2.6–3.1 s), macaws bob (2–2.7 s), the sloths sway from their hands (5.5 s), the quetzal's tail sways (4.5 s). Loops run only on the stops on stage and never under reduced motion; every loop is a `transform`, so nothing relayouts. The specs live in `tools/plates/*.json` (README there).

**Water.** The waterfalls, Río Celeste's rapids and the surf are regions of the plate exported with soft alpha (bright, unsaturated pixels only); two copies translate along the flow and crossfade with a half-period offset so the sum is always one — the oldest trick in the book, and the water never stops. Still water (the Tortuguero channel, the Río Celeste surface) gets a slow shimmer instead.

**The eruption.** At Irazú, "What if it erupted?" (a toggle, reversible) darkens the crater lake, raises an ash column of six puff sprites that drift west (the way the 1963–65 ash went), hazes the sky, drops ash over the valley, shakes the stage once and, if the ambience is on, rumbles. Without motion the column simply stands. "Read what would happen" opens the dossier (research/irazu.json → the sheet): the what-if labelled as interpretation with its basis, the 1723 → 2026 record, who watches the volcano, and the numbers — every item sourced. The copy says in words that it is an interpretation, not a forecast.

**Honesty about the pictures.** Each plate is the owner's composite; the hotspots open sourced species entries and each entry states whether the animal belongs in that scene (`species.stops`): the scarlet macaw drawn over the Tortuguero channel is marked "not quite here" with the evidence (the Caribbean macaw is the great green), and the hummingbirds are labelled as representative species because the drawn birds are generic.

## 5. The motion pass (2026-10-24 evening)

The owner's first reaction to the live walk was "it's not moving". He was right twice over. First, every loop sat inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` — a system setting the owner's own device has had on since the portfolio (DESIGN_MISTAKES, 2026-10-19) — so the animals, the water and the eruption were frozen for him with no way to switch them on; the settings sheet's "Full" did nothing because the media query, not the setting, was the gate. Second, the amplitudes had been tuned for a whisper: a toucan's head tilting 6° and a sloth swaying 2° changed under 0.3 % of their pixels in a second — motion that exists in `getAnimations()` and not for a person.

What changed: the gate is the journey's own calm state, which the visitor's setting overrides, and a notice on the home says plainly that the system asked for stillness and offers the motion with one click. Amplitudes were multiplied (heads ×2.2, wings ×2, sway ×2.6) and every animal got a whole-body loop (perched birds hop and settle, grazers shift their weight, hummingbirds dart, morphos loop wider). The fliers now cross the frame as the stop progresses — the opening macaws fly left over the waterfall, Guanacaste's four toward the sun — with the hotspot ring riding along, and everything leans with the scroll through a smoothed velocity the driver writes (`--vel`). The water runs at twice the amplitude with spray at the waterfalls' feet; the eruption gained a taller column, lightning inside the ash and embers at the vent. Page changes cross with a view transition (the old page recedes, the new one settles) where the browser has them.

Measured in the lab afterwards: 4–37 % of every animal's pixels change within 2.5 s (previously 0–30 %, with the slowest at 0.0 %); the ring stays on the bird across its flight; under an emulated system "reduce motion" the notice appears, and one click brings 23 running loops at Monteverde.

## 6. The alive pass (2026-10-24 night)

Third reaction: "the toucan is terrible", the plates should have moving clouds and wind in the trees, and the scroll lags. The toucan was a motion-pass mistake of mine: the whole-body `perch` loop was applied to animals whose body is still painted into the plate — only the head was ever cut out — so the head rotated and scaled about an invisible body that never moved; the throat line cut straight across. The remedy is in the plates, not the stylesheet: every perched or standing animal now has a `body` part (`rest: true` — the silhouette minus the head), the base is inpainted under it, and only an animal that has one gets a whole-body loop (`bodyAnim` in `Scene.tsx`); the keyframes pivot at the feet so the bird settles on the branch instead of levitating. The opening pair then showed a second fault that had been hiding under the first: the two toucans touch, and each one's segmentation had taken a piece of the other (one body carried the neighbour's body; the other carried the first bird's beak — a second beak that stayed behind when the head turned). `tools/plates.py` now resolves overlapping masks — a poly part wins, otherwise the pixel goes to the animal whose box it lies deeper in, the loser keeping only what the winner leaves — and the spec draws the line between the birds explicitly (`exclude` polygons, an `include` for the beak tip the model lost against the dark neck, a box that holds the whole beak).

For the life of the plates: every plate gained two near-frame foliage layers (the frame polygons that were already in the opening spec, now in all nine) that sway about the frame edge, out of phase, or as one crown where the foliage crosses the split; a cloud strip drifts across a box of each sky; a five-bird flock crosses four of them. All of it rides on the compositor: 200 %-wide strips translated one tile per loop, rotations about the frame edge, two transforms per bird.

For the lag: a scroll frame used to write seven custom properties on the journey root, which recomputed the styles of the whole page (every `calc(var(--pn))` on every stop), then composited full-screen blend modes and repainted the mist's `background-position`. The driver now writes to the elements of the current stop and its neighbours only (`data-scene`), hides stops that are off stage outright, and never touches the root; blend modes are gone; the mist, cloud and shaft fades are baked into their textures instead of CSS masks (each mask was an offscreen surface the GPU filled every frame); the glass buttons on the journey lost their backdrop blur. In the lab the same 60-step scroll pattern went from 30 s of wall time to 5.4 s, the style recalculation from 1.3 s over 130 passes to 1.5 s over 282 passes (three times as many frames fit, each cheaper). A far jump — a rail click, a restored scroll position — also exposed a stale-stop bug (the stop you left stayed visible under the one you landed on while its plate loaded); the driver settles every stop outside its window whenever the current stop changes.

What this lab cannot say is the frame rate on the owner's machine; the `?why` panel is the instrument for that.

## 7. The province pass (2026-10-25)

Ten more images from the owner, and a brief that was mostly a rule: keep what has animals, replace what has none, and let every province have its stop. Two of the old plates had no animals — the Río Celeste pool and the Manuel Antonio sunset — so his new versions take their places (a hummingbird and a morpho at the waterfall; a sea turtle on the beach, which also gives the closing "Why this journey exists" card a living thing to sit beside). Four provinces had no stop at all; they do now — Arenal for Alajuela, the Chirripó páramo for San José, the basilica under Irazú for Cartago, the coffee slopes under Barva for Heredia — and the walk became a route: north to the volcano, west to the cloud forest, back north to the blue river, down to the dry Pacific coast, the table, then the Central Valley from the coffee to the old capital to the crater, up to the roof of the country, and south and east to the two coasts before the Pacific sunset.

The method held: every animal a whole-body cut-out with a head that moves about its neck, every plate with foliage that sways and clouds that drift, every stop with a place record whose numbers carry their sources and their status — including the ones that do not agree (Arenal's 1968 death toll is 78, 87 or "a hundred" depending on who counts; the basilica was finished in 1924, 1930 or 1939) — and a species sheet that says whether the animal belongs there. The Chirripó thrush does not: the drawn bird is the national yigüirro and the páramo's thrush is the sooty thrush, so the sheet says *not quite here* and names the real one, as the Tortuguero macaw's did before it. The turtle's sheet says the drawing looks more like a green turtle than the olive ridley the sources put on that beach. Honesty about the illustration is the price of using it.

Two plates from the first walk survive out of sequence: the Río Celeste pool is now Tenorio's picture on the map page, as the owner asked, and the Manuel Antonio sunset marks Quepos. The tenth image, his composite of the country, is the figure on the "Where I come from" page. The three second versions of existing stops stay in the repository unused; the rule was not to replace a plate that already had animals.

## 8. The clean plates (2026-10-25, afternoon)

The owner asked whether sending the images without their animals would make the animation better. It does, for one reason: every cut-out had been moving over a hole I inpainted, so each loop could only travel as far as the patch behind it stayed hidden — heads turned 8–13°, nobody hopped. He erased the animals from twelve of the images with his tool's generative fill and sent the results; they are pixel-aligned with the originals (0.95–0.99 correlation, 86–95 % of pixels unchanged — the rest is the fill under the animals and the generator's re-encoding noise), so eight plates on the walk now sit on his own clean scene: Arenal, Río Celeste, the closing beach, the coffee highlands, Cartago, Chirripó, Guanacaste and Corcovado. Nothing is inpainted on those plates; the animals are still cut from the originals, and the difference between the two files is used only as a veto on the rim of a cut-out, never to cut into it. The clean bases also showed an old error the inpainting had hidden: three hummingbirds (coffee, both at Cartago) had been carrying a square of sky under the bill since the province pass, because each bill's include polygon sat a few pixels too low; the bills are now cut on their measured pixel rows.

With the hole gone the loops travel. On a clean plate the heads turn further (the body part now keeps a band under the head, so a turn opens onto neck rather than onto the base), the Arenal toucan and the two thrushes hop along their perch and back, the deer and the tapir shift their weight more, hummingbirds and morphos roam wider, and the turtle crawls toward the surf as the stop scrolls (the ring follows it) while pushing with its flippers in a 3.6 s loop. The four plates without a clean scene yet (the opening forest, Monteverde, Irazú, Tortuguero) keep their inpainted bases and the earlier, narrower loops. A thirteenth image the owner sent for the opening turned out to be a new generation of the scene, not an erase — the volcano and the branches had moved — so it could not be used: the toucans would have sat on branches that were not there.

## 9. One toucan, and the opening on the new scene (2026-10-25, late afternoon)

The owner's first look at the clean build came with four notes: the deer's antlers did not move with its head; the hummingbirds' bills were "a black line" (left behind in the inpainted base); the opening toucans had a border and the head move "looks like he is cracking his head open"; and the background should be the version without the animals, with the animals added back on top — which is what the eight clean stops already were. Then: "first page keep only one toucan".

The opening is now his regenerated forest (the thirteenth image, which could not serve as an erase of the old scene) used as a clean base, with the animals of the first opening plate pasted onto it by a new `paste` mode of the plate tool: one keel-billed toucan on the big mossy branch at the lower left (its feet on the moss, a hop along the branch), the sloth hanging from the vines at the upper right, the two macaws over the valley left of the volcano, the morpho over the ferns at the lower right. Nothing is inpainted, so the border and the gap at the throat are gone; the head tilt is lighter everywhere (6° on the inpainted plates, 7° on the clean ones, 3.6 s), with smaller secondary moves. The deer's antlers are in its head part (the difference between the clean and the original images finds them against the sky, where the segmentation lost them). The Irazú hummingbird's bill is cut with the bird.

## 10. The opening on the author's third pair (2026-10-25, evening)

Half an hour after the one-toucan opening, the owner sent a third scene for the first page: a new picture (a bromeliad-and-orchid frame, the volcano over a lake, a waterfall into a turquoise river, the sunrise at the right) in two versions — with its animals and without — and asked that it replace the home page, that the animals move, that the scroll feel like zooming and walking into the journey, with the waterfall flowing. The pair is pixel-aligned (0.91 correlation at 320 px; the difference is the animals and the generator's re-encoding), so the plate is built the clean way: the base is his empty scene, the animals are cut from the full one — one toucan (his standing rule), the sloth above it, two hummingbirds, a red-eyed tree frog on its leaf, two scarlet macaws flying toward the sun, the morpho — with no inpainting anywhere. The waterfall, the rapids below it, the pool and the lake move. The walk-in is the scroll: the plate zooms 22 % toward the waterfall through the stop while the near foliage parts and passes the camera, the macaws and the morpho cross the valley, the hummingbirds dart at their flowers.

The second opening (the regenerated scene with the first plate's cut-outs pasted on) is kept as `forest-v2.json` for the record, not built.
